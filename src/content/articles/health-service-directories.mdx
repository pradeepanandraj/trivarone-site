<p class="lede">
  Most practices think about being found in two places: Google, and
  increasingly, whatever answer an AI assistant gives when somebody asks it for
  a recommendation.
</p>

<p>
  There is a third place, and it sits underneath both. It is a national
  directory of health services, run by a Commonwealth agency, and your practice
  may already be in it with details you have never checked.
</p>

<h2>The healthdirect Service Finder</h2>

<p>
  The Service Finder is the consumer-facing search tool of the National Health
  Services Directory, run by Healthdirect Australia. It covers general
  practices, pharmacies, dentists, psychologists, pathology, optical and a wide
  range of allied health services.
</p>

<p>
  Healthdirect states the Service Finder receives over a million searches each
  month.
</p>

<p>
  That figure is worth pausing on. It is not the most important thing about this
  directory.
</p>

<h2>The part that is not marketing</h2>

<p>
  NHSD data is used inside clinical systems.
</p>

<p>
  Healthdirect states the data supports clinical workflows, including helping
  healthcare professionals identify suitable providers for referrals, and
  enabling discharge summaries to reach a patient's regular provider. Hospitals
  draw practice contact details from it.
</p>

<p>
  Read that again with a stale listing in mind.
</p>

<blockquote>
  <p>An out-of-date NHSD entry is not a missed search result. It is a referral sent to a phone number that changed two years ago. It is a discharge summary that does not arrive, for a patient who assumes their practice knows they were in hospital.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>
  This is the reason the Service Finder is worth ten minutes of a practice
  manager's attention even if not one patient ever uses it to find you.
</p>

<h2>How a listing gets there, and who maintains it</h2>

<p>
  There are two routes in. A practice can register directly with the NHSD, or
  register with Provider Connect Australia, which allows a practice to maintain
  its business information in one place and publish it to the Service Finder and
  other partners. Healthdirect states that a practice registering through
  Provider Connect Australia can publish to the Service Finder the same day.
</p>

<p>
  Both are free.
</p>

<p>
  Maintenance sits with the practice, not with Healthdirect. Healthdirect states
  that healthcare services are responsible for maintaining their listings, and
  recommends reviewing them at least every twelve months. A listing that is
  already public can be corrected using the "suggest an edit" button on the
  listing itself.
</p>

<p>
  That division of responsibility is the whole problem in one sentence. The
  directory is national infrastructure. The accuracy of your entry in it is your
  job, and nobody will tell you when it stops being right.
</p>

<p>
  One further point for general practices. From November 2025, practices
  participating in the Commonwealth's Bulk Billing Practice Incentive Payment
  program were required to list their bulk-billing services in the NHSD, making
  them visible in Service Finder.
</p>

<h2>Why a directory listing matters for AI search</h2>

<p>
  A listing is not only a place a person might click.
</p>

<p>
  When an AI system is asked to recommend a service, it does not only read the
  practice's own website. It draws on what other sources say about you. A
  practice named consistently across a Commonwealth health directory, a
  professional register and its own site is a more legible entity than one that
  exists only on its own domain.
</p>

<p>
  This is the part that is easy to miss. Work on your own website is work on the
  one source you control. Directory listings are work on the sources you do not,
  and those are the ones that establish a practice as a real thing in the world
  rather than as a claim it makes about itself.
</p>

<h2>What to actually do</h2>

<p>
  Search the
  <a href="https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/australian-health-services" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">healthdirect Service Finder</a>
  for your own practice name. Not your profession, and not your suburb — your
  name.
</p>

<p>
  Then ask three questions about what comes back.
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Are you there at all.</li>
  <li>Is the phone number the one you would want a hospital to use.</li>
  <li>Are the practitioners listed the ones who still work there.</li>
</ul>

<p>
  None of this requires a marketing budget. Most of it requires an afternoon.
</p>
