Why your business ranks for its own name but not for what you sell

Pradeep Anandraj · 7 August 2026 · 6 minute read

Almost every business appears when somebody searches its own name. Far fewer appear when somebody searches the thing they sell. This is about the gap between the two, why it happens, and how to find out which side of it you are on.

Search your business name. Then search what you sell. If the first works and the second does not, you have the most common problem I see, and almost nobody knows they have it.

What does the second search actually tell you?

The first search is close to meaningless. If somebody types your business name into Google, they already know you exist. They have been referred, or they met you, or they saw your van. Appearing for your own name means you have not made a mistake. It does not mean you are findable.

The second search is the one that matters, because it is the search a person makes when they need what you do but have no idea who you are. Not "Northside Physio" but "physio for shoulder pain near Chermside". Not your name but your category.

Those two searches produce completely different results, and the gap between them is where most of the lost work sits.

What happened when I ran this on a real business?

Last week I looked at a business that ranks first for its own name. The listing was accurate. Five star rating. A website better built than most of its competitors'. Eight detailed guides published on it. Nothing had been neglected.

Then I searched the category it trades in, across five places: Google, Google Maps, Google's AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity.

Nineteen competitors appeared across those five searches. The business did not appear once.

Not once in five. Not buried on the second page. Absent.

What does it mean if your business does not appear?

It means the business can be found by people who already know its name, and by nobody else.

Everyone who was referred will find it. Everyone who met the owner will find it. Everyone who searched for the service without knowing who provides it went to one of the nineteen.

That is a business running entirely on word of mouth without knowing it is doing so. Which works, until the referrals slow down.

What usually causes a business not to appear for its own category?

This is the part that surprised the owner, because none of it was about the website he had spent money on.

CauseWhat it wasCost to fix
A single listing setting One field on the business listing, set in a way that limited where the business could appear Free. Under an hour
Review volume Thirteen reviews. Competitors appearing in those searches held over two hundred Free, but takes months
Age of the business Nine months old. Google has not accumulated enough signal to trust it yet Cannot be fixed. Only waited out

Two of the three were free to fix. The third could not be fixed at all, only outlasted.

Notice what is not on that list. Not the website. Not the content. Not the design. The owner had done the visible work well and was invisible anyway, because the causes sat somewhere he had never been told to look.

Why does this matter more now than it did two years ago?

Because two of those five searches were not a list of links. Google's AI Mode and ChatGPT return an answer, and an answer names a small number of businesses.

A page of search results has ten positions and a second page. An AI answer names three or four, and there is no second page. Being eleventh used to mean being nearly there. Being outside the named few now means not existing for that question.

How do you run this test on your own business?

It takes about thirty seconds and you do not need anybody's help.

  • Search your business name. Confirm you appear. You almost certainly will.
  • Search the service you sell plus your suburb, with your business name left out entirely.
  • Do the second search in more than one place. Google, then ChatGPT, then Perplexity, and see whether the answers agree.
  • Write down who appears instead of you.

That last step is the useful one. A list of the businesses being put in front of your customers is more informative than any score a tool will give you, because those are the businesses you are actually losing work to.

What should you do if you are not there?

Do not start with the website. That is almost always the most expensive thing to change and rarely the cause.

Start with the business listing, because a single misconfigured field is the most common single cause and it costs nothing to correct. Then look at review volume against the businesses that did appear, which is a number you can count in five minutes. Then check whether your site can be read by AI systems at all, which is a separate thirty-second check.

And if the answer turns out to be that the business is simply too young, that is worth knowing too. It stops you spending money trying to fix something that only time fixes.

The test costs you thirty seconds

Search your name. Then search what you sell. Almost everyone passes the first test. The second is the one that tells you something.

The business described here is real and has been anonymised. Findings were recorded in July 2026 across Google, Google Maps, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity. Search and AI systems change their behaviour frequently, and the same searches run today may return different results.
