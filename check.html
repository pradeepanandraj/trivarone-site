Search your business name. Then search what you sell.

What it involves

Two searches, five places

Two searches are run for your business across five places: Google, Google Maps, Google's AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity.

The first is for your business name. The second is for the service you sell, with your name left out entirely.

Almost every business passes the first. The second is where the answer is, because that is the search a person makes when they need what you do but do not yet know who you are.

  1. Whether you appear at all

    Across all five surfaces, for your own category. Some businesses appear in one and not the other four. Some appear in none.

  2. Who appears instead of you

    Named. Not a count, not a percentage — the actual businesses being put in front of the person who was looking for what you sell.

  3. What is most likely causing it

    Usually two or three things, and usually not what the owner expects. A single setting on a business listing. Review volume against competitors. A site an AI system cannot read. Age.

  4. What is free to fix, and what is not

    Separated plainly, so you can act on the free ones yourself without paying anybody, including me.

How it works

What is asked of you

Your business name, your website, and the service you would most want to be found for. That is the whole intake. No form of twenty questions, no discovery call, no access to any of your accounts.

The check is returned as a short written summary, usually within a few days. It is written to be read once and understood, not to demonstrate how much work went into it.

There is no obligation attached and no follow-up sequence. If the findings are things you can fix yourself, that is a good outcome and you should go and fix them.

Worth saying plainly

What this is not

It is not an automated report. The searches are run by hand and the findings are written by a person. That is slower than a tool and it is the reason the findings are worth reading.

It is not a score. A number out of a hundred tells you nothing you can act on. The check tells you what is happening and what is causing it.

It does not come with a guarantee. Nobody controls what Google or ChatGPT says about a business, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. What can be controlled is whether the information those systems rely on is accurate, complete and readable.
